# Tone and Copywriting

Voice is constant. Tone is not. The same brand personality speaks differently on a product label than in a crisis email. This file governs how tone shifts across contexts and how to write copy that stays on-brand at every register.

---

## The Tone Spectrum

Every brand voice operates across a range of tone. Define the range — not just the center.

| Context | Tone shifts toward | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Social media** | Casual, reactive, compressed | Audience is scrolling. You have one thumb-stop. |
| **Email / newsletter** | Conversational, direct, personal | Reader opted in. Reward the attention. |
| **Website / landing page** | Confident, benefit-led, scannable | Visitor is evaluating. Answer "why should I care?" fast. |
| **Packaging / product** | Sensory, tactile, warm | The customer is holding the thing. Speak to their hands. |
| **Customer service** | Calm, clear, human | Someone has a problem. Empathy first, solution second. |
| **Legal / compliance** | Precise, neutral, unambiguous | Clarity protects both parties. No personality required. |
| **Internal / hiring** | Honest, aspirational, grounded | Employees detect bullshit faster than customers. |
| **Press / PR** | Authoritative, quotable, concise | Journalists will cut your sentence in half. Front-load meaning. |

**Named rule — The Tone Bracket:** Before writing, name the context and its tone position. "This is a social post — casual end of our range." If you cannot name the position, you will drift.

## Copywriting Rules by Brand Personality Type

### The Warm Brand (artisanal, human, community)
- First person plural ("we") or second person ("you"). Never third person.
- Contractions always. "We're" not "We are."
- Sensory details over abstractions. "Sun-dried on coastal racks" not "premium quality."
- Short paragraphs. Let white space do work.
- Permission to be imperfect — a casual aside, an unfinished thought.

### The Authority Brand (expert, institutional, trusted)
- Declarative sentences. Subject-verb-object. No hedging.
- Data and specifics over adjectives. "47 countries since 1983" not "global reach."
- Minimal exclamation marks — confidence doesn't shout.
- Longer sentences are acceptable if the structure is clean.
- Attribution matters — cite sources, name researchers, show the work.

### The Challenger Brand (bold, defiant, disruptive)
- Short sentences. Fragments. One-word paragraphs.
- Direct address: "You know this is broken. So do we."
- Permission to be provocative, not offensive.
- Break grammar rules on purpose, consistently. ("Think different." "Just do it.")
- The enemy is always the status quo, never a named competitor.

### The Minimal Brand (restrained, precise, quiet)
- Every word must earn its place. Cut until it hurts, then cut one more.
- No adjectives that the noun already implies.
- White space is part of the copy. What you leave out says as much.
- Lowercase where conventions permit.
- Rhythm matters more than content volume.

## Same Voice, Different Tone — Worked Example

A craft coffee roastery with voice traits: *weathered, aromatic, unhurried.*

| Context | Copy |
|---|---|
| **Packaging** | Slow-roasted in small batches. Rested fourteen days before grinding. Some things shouldn't be rushed. |
| **Instagram caption** | Morning light through the roastery windows. Today's batch: Kenyan Nyeri, natural process, rested two weeks. Worth the wait. |
| **Email subject** | Your beans shipped this morning |
| **Email body** | We roasted your Nyeri on Tuesday, let it rest until Thursday, and shipped it today. It'll be at its best between days 5 and 21 after roast. No rush — it'll wait for you. |
| **Customer complaint response** | That's not the experience we want for you. We're sending a fresh bag today — no need to return the other one. If the new batch still tastes off, let us know and we'll dig into what happened. |
| **404 page** | This page went the way of a forgotten French press. Let's get you back to something worth brewing. |

The voice (weathered, aromatic, unhurried) holds across every example. The tone ranges from intimate (packaging) to practical (email) to light (404).

## The "Is This On-Brand?" Test

Run every piece of copy through these five checks in order. Fail any one, rewrite.

### 1. Voice check
Read the copy aloud. Does it sound like the brand's personality words? If "weathered, aromatic, unhurried" — does the copy feel rushed, generic, or sterile? If yes, it has left the voice.

### 2. Tone check
Is the tone appropriate for this context? A joke in a refund email is tone-deaf. Corporate stiffness in an Instagram story is tone-deaf. Name the context, verify the register.

### 3. Substitution check
Replace your brand name with a competitor's. Does the copy still work? If yes, it is not distinctive. Rewrite until the substitution breaks.

### 4. Banned-word check
Scan for any word on the banned list in voice.md. One banned word contaminates the paragraph. Replace with a concrete, specific alternative.

### 5. Audience check
Would the target audience actually speak this way to a friend? If the copy uses words the audience would never say aloud, the brand is talking at them, not with them.

## Copy Templates by Tone

These are structural scaffolds, not fill-in-the-blanks. Adapt to voice.

### Announcement (confident tone)
**Structure:** What happened → Why it matters to you → What to do next.
Keep to three sentences. The first carries the news. The second earns attention. The third gives a path forward.

### Story (warm tone)
**Structure:** Sensory opening → Tension or contrast → Resolution that reveals brand values.
Never open with the brand name. Open with a moment the reader can see, hear, or taste.

### Sell (direct tone)
**Structure:** Pain or desire → Product as answer → Single call to action.
One CTA per piece. Two CTAs halve the conversion of each. Three CTAs produce paralysis.

### Educate (authority tone)
**Structure:** Question the reader has → Answer with specificity → Implication they hadn't considered.
Teach something real. If the reader learns nothing, it was marketing dressed as education.

## Tone Slop Test

If the copy could be described as "professional and engaging," it has no tone. Those words describe the absence of a decision, not the presence of a voice.

**First-order check:** Can you name the specific tone position from the spectrum table above? "Casual-end social" or "precise-end compliance"? If you reach for "friendly yet professional," start over.

**Second-order check:** Does the copy contain any sentence that works equally well for every brand in this category? That sentence is filler. Replace it with something only this brand would say.

## Bans

- **The double greeting.** "Hi there! Welcome!" — one greeting per communication. Pick one.
- **The false question.** "Ready to elevate your morning?" — rhetorical questions that assume the answer are manipulative, not conversational.
- **The everything sentence.** "We're passionate about delivering innovative, sustainable, community-driven solutions." — stacking values into one sentence dilutes all of them. One value per sentence.
- **The mirror compliment.** "You deserve the best." — flattery without specificity is transparent.
- **The urgency fabrication.** "Don't miss out!" when nothing is scarce. Artificial urgency erodes trust in one use.

---

## Permissions

- Tone **may** include humor if the brand voice supports it and the context is appropriate. Humor on a 404 page is generous. Humor in a data breach notification is reckless.
- Tone **may** be informal on social platforms even for authority brands — but informality means relaxed structure, not abandoned standards.
- Tone **may** use sentence fragments, single-word paragraphs, and unconventional punctuation if these choices are consistent and documented in the style sheet.
- Tone **may** shift sharply between contexts. The same brand can be playful on Instagram and precise in a contract. The voice underneath must hold.
