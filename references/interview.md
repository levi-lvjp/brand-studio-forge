# Brand Interview and Discovery


---

## Slop Test

If your interview is a form with dropdowns, you'll get form answers. No insight.
If you ask "what three adjectives describe your brand" without probing what those adjectives mean in concrete behavior, you've collected noise.
If every interview produces the same brand brief shape regardless of the business, you're templating, not discovering.

---

## Interview Principles

### Discovery Rules

1. **Face-to-face is best.** Recording facilitates eye contact and better follow-up. If not possible, virtual is acceptable. Phone is last resort.
2. **No questions in advance.** Spontaneous answers are more insightful. (Exception: very senior executives who insist.)
3. **Keep it under 45 minutes.**
4. **Read baseline information first.** Review all company documents before the interview. Convey that you've done your homework.
5. **Balance structure and flow.** Strike a balance between sticking to your questions and allowing the interviewee to steer the conversation toward their areas of expertise.
6. **Co-create the interviewee list** with the client. Include leadership, employees at different levels, and customers when possible.

### Deep-Discovery Rules

1. **Start by finding where the brand stands, not where you want it to go.**
2. **The biggest temptation is to declare you know exactly what the problem is.** Resist.
3. **Balance internal desire with external reality.** Organizations often have an imbalance between self-perception and outside perception.
4. **Hear many views. Air disagreements without naming names.** Then take polar views into strategy.

## Core Interview Questions

### Foundation Questions

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 1 | What business are you in? |
| 2 | What is your mission? What are your three most important goals? |
| 3 | Why was this company created? |
| 4 | Describe your products or services. |
| 5 | Who is your target market? |
| 6 | Prioritize your stakeholders in order of importance. How do you want to be perceived by each audience? |
| 7 | What is your competitive advantage? Why do customers choose you? What do you do better than anyone else? |
| 8 | Who is your competition? Is there a competitor you admire? Why? |
| 9 | How do you market your products or services? |

### Strategic Questions

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 10 | What are the major trends and changes that affect your industry? |
| 11 | Where will you be in five years? In ten years? |
| 12 | How do you measure success? |
| 13 | What values and beliefs unify your employees and drive their performance? |
| 14 | What are the potential barriers to the success of your products or services? |
| 15 | What keeps you up at night? |

### Vision Questions

| # | Question |
|---|---|
| 16 | Place yourself in the future. If your company could do anything or be anything, what would it be? |
| 17 | If you could communicate a single message about your company, what would it be? |

## Deep-Dive Question Banks

### Questions to Ponder

**Peter Drucker:** What is your business? Who is the customer? What is the value to the customer? What will our business be? What should our business be?

**Keith Yamashita (SYPartners):** Why do we exist? What will we become? What makes employees passionate about their work? What excites our customers? What are the ideas that drive our company? What are we doing that's different from what everyone else in our industry is doing? What do we need to be successful? What is holding us back?

**Jim Collins (Good to Great):** What are you passionate about? What can you be best in the world at? What drives your economic engine?

**Basecamp:** Why are we doing this? What problem are we solving? Is this actually useful? Are we adding value? Will this change behavior? Is there an easier way? What's the opportunity cost? Is it really worth it?

### Three-Layer Questions

**Layer 1 — Functional (nuts and bolts):**
- What is it that you do?
- How do you do it?
- What about your approach differs from others?

**Layer 2 — Emotional (personality and values):**
- Are you clear what this organization believes in?
- What does it value most?
- What kind of personality does it have? Does that change for different audiences?

**Layer 3 — Purpose (why we're here):**
- What have you been put on this earth to do?
- What problem do you exist to solve?
- What's your long-term ambition, and why will it matter in the future?
- What does success look like?

## Audit Methodology

### Five Audit Types

| Audit | What to Examine | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | Existing identity across all surfaces | See where you are. Highlight inconsistencies. |
| **Verbal** | Words, phrases, messaging, tone | Stepping stone to improve language or trigger complete change |
| **Behavioural** | How employees speak, interact, deliver | Conscious and unconscious signals about the brand |
| **Competition** | All factors above, across key competitors | Map the landscape |
| **Peer** | Aspirational organizations, often cross-sector | Benchmarks and possibilities |

### Marketing Audit Process

A methodical examination of all marketing communications and identity systems, past and present.

**Steps:**
1. Understand the big picture (markets, sales, distribution, challenges)
2. Request materials (existing and archival identity, sales materials, collateral, signage, packaging)
3. Create a system (organize, retrieve, document, review)
4. Solicit information (contextual background, attitudes toward brand and identity)
5. Examine materials (sales, collateral, communications, environments, products)

### Competitive Audit Process

**Steps:**
1. Identify competitors (leading, category-adjacent, indirect)
2. Gather information and research
3. Determine positioning (competitive positioning, features/benefits, strengths/weaknesses, personality, category)
4. Identify key messages (mission, tagline, descriptors, themes)
5. Examine visual identity (symbols, color, typography, look and feel)

**Understanding the Competition checklist:**
- Who are they? What do their brands stand for?
- What markets/audiences do they serve?
- What advantages (strengths) do they have? Disadvantages (weaknesses)?
- How do they sell and cultivate customers?
- How do they position themselves?
- What are their key messages?
- How do they use brand identity to succeed?
- What do they look and feel like?

## Named Rules

### The Simple Mapping Rule

Three lightweight tools that work in discovery sessions:

1. **SWOT** — Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats on one page
2. **Facts / Obstacles / Opportunities** — three columns, one page, identifies key issues
3. **Two-by-Two brand map** — plot brands on simple axes (e.g. "economy ↔ premium" vs. "personal ↔ impersonal"). Reveals white space.

### The Perception Timeline Rule

Plot the brand's core attributes across three time horizons: **Past → Present → Future**. Lets employees critique direction without personal attribution.

### The Findings Report Rule

Synthesize all interviews, audits, and research into a formal presentation. Organize across five dimensions: Interviews, Brand essence, Marketing research, Marketing audit, Verbal audit.

---

## Bans

- Do not provide questions in advance unless the interviewee insists.
- Do not use focus groups as a replacement for the decision-making process.
- Do not skip the competitive audit. If you haven't mapped competitors, you can't differentiate.
- Do not conduct audits without documenting findings. Undocumented research is wasted research.
- Do not confuse personal aesthetics with functional criteria during audits.

---

## Permissions

- You may merge all five audit types into a single lightweight pass for solo founders with no existing brand assets.
- You may run the interview in multiple short sessions rather than one long one.
- You may add domain-specific questions beyond the core set based on the business category.
- You may use the three-layer structure (functional → emotional → purpose) as an alternative to the linear question list.
