{{BRAND_NAME}} Color
02 Color Palette
Our palette is defined in OKLCH for perceptual uniformity. Chroma reduces as lightness approaches extremes. Every neutral is tinted toward the brand hue.
{{COLOR_PALETTE}}
Usage Proportions
Primary carries 30 to 60 percent of brand surfaces. Use on key actions, headlines, and brand moments.
Secondary supports. Use on accents, secondary UI, and complementary surfaces.
Neutrals dominate backgrounds and body text. Always tinted, never pure grey.
All values specified in OKLCH (Lightness, Chroma, Hue). Convert to sRGB hex for legacy systems only when required. Verify gamut mapping on target devices.

{{BRAND_NAME}} Imagery
05 Imagery
Photography and illustration carry the brand as much as color and type. Consistency in visual treatment keeps the identity coherent across every touchpoint.
{{IMAGERY_PHOTOGRAPHY_TAG}}
{{IMAGERY_ILLUSTRATION_TAG}}
Photography
Natural light. Unstaged, lived-in moments. Muted color grading that respects the brand palette. Subjects feel present, not performing.
Illustration
Drawn from the brand color palette. Line weight consistent with type weight. Spare, not decorative. Illustration should clarify, not ornament.
Composition
Off-center subjects. Generous negative space. Allow the image to breathe within the layout rather than filling every available pixel.
What to avoid
Stock photography with artificial poses. Oversaturated color. Overly processed filters. Clip art. Generic icon sets. Decorative elements that carry no meaning.

{{BRAND_NAME}} Guidelines
06 Do's & Don'ts
Quick reference for applying the brand correctly. When in doubt, return to these principles.
Do
Use the brand mark at prescribed sizes with proper clear space
Apply the OKLCH palette as specified, tinting neutrals toward brand hue
Pair the display typeface with the body typeface as documented
Write in our voice: consistent, recognizable, true to the brand
Use natural, unstaged photography with muted tones
Leave generous whitespace in layouts
Test color reproduction across screen and print
Don't
Stretch, rotate, recolor, or add effects to the mark
Introduce colors outside the defined palette
Substitute typefaces without approval
Use generic corporate language or AI-branding tells
Use stock photography with staged or artificial compositions
Crowd layouts with competing elements
Reproduce the mark below minimum size specifications
A brand is not a logo. It is the entire experience, made coherent.
