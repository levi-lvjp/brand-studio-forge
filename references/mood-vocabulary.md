# Mood Vocabulary

Map from brand mood to concrete design decisions. When the interview produces personality words, translate them through this reference to reach specific color, type, layout, voice, and texture choices.

Do not pick from this reference by keyword-matching alone. A brand that says "bold" might mean loud-and-colorful or might mean confident-and-restrained. The interview context determines which archetype fits.

---

## 1. Commanding

The brand leads. It does not ask permission. Authority without aggression.

- **OKLCH range:** L 15–35 (deep darks), C 20–50 (restrained saturation), H anchored — one dominant hue
- **Color strategy:** Committed — one saturated color on dark surfaces
- **Typography:** High-contrast serif or heavy grotesque. Large size differential between heading and body (3:1 minimum). Tight tracking on uppercase headings.
- **Safe fonts:** Styrene, Suisse Intl, Freight Display, GT Sectra, Druk
- **Logo style:** Wordmark in custom or heavy-weight type. No playful marks. If a symbol exists, it's dense and geometric.
- **Voice traits:** Declarative sentences. Short paragraphs. No hedging ("we believe" → state the thing). No exclamation marks.
- **Banned words:** "excited," "thrilled," "fun," "journey"
- **Texture:** Matte, dense, zero grain. Solid color fields. If photography, high contrast with deep shadows.
- **Reference brands:** Aesop, Acne Studios, Bloomberg, The Economist

---

## 2. Warm

The brand embraces. It feels like a friend who knows what they're doing.

- **OKLCH range:** L 55–80 (middle-light), C 30–60, H 40–90 (ambers, terracottas, warm ochres)
- **Color strategy:** Full palette — 3–4 warm tones with deliberate roles
- **Typography:** Humanist sans or transitional serif with round terminals. Generous line-height (1.6–1.75). Mixed case, never all-caps for body text.
- **Safe fonts:** Söhne, Untitled Sans, Tiempos Text, Spectral, Source Serif 4
- **Logo style:** Wordmark with slight weight variation or a rounded mark. Approachable geometry — circles and soft corners over sharp angles.
- **Voice traits:** Second person ("you" centered). Contractions. Conversational but not sloppy. Addresses the reader directly.
- **Banned words:** "leverage," "optimize," "synergy," "stakeholder"
- **Texture:** Paper grain, canvas, linen. Slightly imperfect surfaces. Warm photography with visible natural light.
- **Reference brands:** Airbnb, Mailchimp (pre-Intuit), Patagonia, Kinfolk

---

## 3. Precise

The brand measures twice. Every element is justified. Craft as ethos.

- **OKLCH range:** L 85–97 (near-white surfaces), C 5–15 (barely tinted), plus one accent at C 40–70
- **Color strategy:** Restrained — tinted neutrals + single accent ≤10% surface area
- **Typography:** Grotesque sans with consistent stroke widths. Monospaced for data or labels. Tight margins, generous tracking on small text. Tabular figures in any numerical context.
- **Safe fonts:** Graphik, Founders Grotesk, Atlas Grotesk, JetBrains Mono, Pitch
- **Logo style:** Geometric wordmark, letterspaced. If a mark exists, it's built on exact geometric relationships (golden ratio, grid-locked). Clear minimum-size and clearspace rules.
- **Voice traits:** Active voice. Specific numbers over vague claims ("12 ms" not "fast"). No superlatives. Technical terms used accurately.
- **Banned words:** "revolutionary," "game-changing," "best-in-class," "world-class"
- **Texture:** Flat, clean. No grain. No texture. If photography, studio-lit on neutral backgrounds. Data visualizations are a first-class design element.
- **Reference brands:** Stripe, Linear, Teenage Engineering, Braun

---

## 4. Rebellious

The brand breaks convention on purpose, not by accident. Controlled chaos.

- **OKLCH range:** High contrast — L 10–25 paired with L 85–100. C 60–100 (full saturation for accents). Acid tones welcome: H 80–130 (yellows/chartreuse), H 330–360 (magentas).
- **Color strategy:** Drenched — the surface IS the color. Or committed with a confrontational accent.
- **Typography:** Condensed or wide extremes. Clashing pairings (compressed sans + wide serif). Oversized display type that crowds the margins. Broken baselines are allowed.
- **Safe fonts:** Neue Haas Unica, ABC Diatype, Monument Extended, Redaction, NB International
- **Logo style:** Custom lettering or radical type treatment. Imperfect geometry. The mark should feel like it was torn, stamped, stenciled, or screen-printed — not rendered.
- **Voice traits:** Short fragments. Imperative mood ("Do this. Skip that."). Breaks grammar rules intentionally. Addresses the establishment by name.
- **Banned words:** "premium," "luxury," "curated," "bespoke"
- **Texture:** High grain, halftone, noise, concrete, newsprint. If photography, candid, flash-lit, off-center. Deliberate imperfection.
- **Reference brands:** Supreme, Vetements, The Intercept, Liquid Death

---

## 5. Serene

The brand calms. It creates space, not noise.

- **OKLCH range:** L 80–95 (light, airy), C 5–25 (desaturated), H 180–240 (cool blues, sage greens, stone)
- **Color strategy:** Restrained — near-monochromatic palette with one quiet accent
- **Typography:** Thin or light weight humanist sans or calligraphic serif. Generous line-height (1.7+). Wide margins. Never condensed or heavy.
- **Safe fonts:** Cera Pro, Canela, Freight Text, GT Alpina, Blanco
- **Logo style:** Lightweight wordmark with generous letterspacing. If a symbol, organic and open — leaf shapes, waves, open circles. Never dense or enclosed.
- **Voice traits:** Long sentences allowed. Unhurried rhythm. Descriptive. Sensory language ("the weight of linen," "morning light on stone"). No urgency.
- **Banned words:** "disrupt," "hack," "crush," "dominate," "grind"
- **Texture:** Smooth gradients (single-hue, subtle), watercolor washes, paper, fabric. If photography, soft focus or wide landscapes with high key lighting.
- **Reference brands:** Aesop (skincare line), Muji, Cereal Magazine, Wildsmith

---

## 6. Playful

The brand has fun without being childish. Wit, not whimsy.

- **OKLCH range:** L 55–75, C 50–80 (saturated but not neon), H distributed — use 3+ hues separated by ≥60° on the hue wheel
- **Color strategy:** Full palette — multiple named colors, each with a personality
- **Typography:** Rounded sans or grotesque with quirky details (ink traps, irregular counters). Large, bold display type. Mix weights dramatically within a line for emphasis.
- **Safe fonts:** Roc Grotesk, General Sans, Recoleta, Quincy CF, Clash Display
- **Logo style:** Wordmark with typographic play — ligatures, custom characters, integrated illustrations. The mark should have a visual gag or surprise element.
- **Voice traits:** Contractions always. Pop culture references. Self-aware humor. Short paragraphs. Questions directed at the reader. Punctuation used expressively (em dashes, ellipses).
- **Banned words:** "solutions," "enterprise," "robust," "scalable"
- **Texture:** Flat illustration, bold outlines, halftone dots, sticker/badge aesthetic. If photography, candid with high saturation. Motion: bouncy easing, playful transitions.
- **Reference brands:** Notion (original), Duolingo, Figma, Oatly

---

## 7. Heritage

The brand has roots. It honors tradition without being stuck in it.

- **OKLCH range:** L 25–55 (mid-darks), C 15–40 (muted, aged), H 20–60 (oxblood, tobacco, forest, navy — colors that look good worn-in)
- **Color strategy:** Committed — one deep tone carries the brand, supported by aged neutrals
- **Typography:** Old-style serif or humanist serif. Medium weight, never thin. Text figures (old-style numerals). Small caps for labels. Justified body text with careful hyphenation.
- **Safe fonts:** Galaxie Copernicus, Whitman, Lyon Display, Chronicle Display, Caslon (authentic cuts)
- **Logo style:** Typographic, often in a badge or crest form. Justified or centered lockup. Monogram possible if the brand has actual history. Avoid faux-heritage marks for brands younger than 20 years.
- **Voice traits:** Third person or editorial "we." Longer sentences allowed. Historical references. Active voice but measured pace. Proper nouns capitalized formally.
- **Banned words:** "disrupt," "pivot," "startup," "hustle," "growth hack"
- **Texture:** Leather, embossing, foil, engraving, woven. If photography, directional light with rich shadows. Print: uncoated paper stock.
- **Reference brands:** J.Crew (heritage era), Filson, The New Yorker, Barbour

---

## 8. Minimal

The brand reduces. Every element earns its place or gets cut.

- **OKLCH range:** L 92–98 (near-white) with L 10–20 (near-black) for text. C ≤5. One accent if any, at C 30–50.
- **Color strategy:** Restrained — near-achromatic with one optional accent
- **Typography:** Neo-grotesque or geometric sans. One family, two weights maximum. Tight tracking on uppercase. Generous margins are mandatory — white space IS the brand.
- **Safe fonts:** Haas Grotesk, Neue Montreal, Aktiv Grotesk, Suisse Intl, Unica77
- **Logo style:** Logotype only — no icon mark. Letterspaced uppercase or title case in the brand typeface. If a mark is absolutely required, reduce to the simplest possible geometric form.
- **Voice traits:** Short sentences. No adjectives that don't add information. Remove every word you can remove. If the sentence still works, remove another.
- **Banned words:** "passionate," "dedicated," "committed," "excellence," "holistic"
- **Texture:** None. Flat surfaces, no grain, no shadows, no gradients. If photography, isolated subjects on solid backgrounds. Product photography only.
- **Reference brands:** COS, Byredo, Jil Sander, Tobias van Schneider

---

## 9. Raw

The brand shows its working. Unpolished is the point.

- **OKLCH range:** L 85–97 (paper white) + L 5–20 (near black) as primary pair. Limited palette — one or two spot colors at full saturation (C 70+), used sparingly.
- **Color strategy:** Restrained but with one drenched accent — the contrast IS the brand
- **Typography:** Monospaced or industrial sans. Exposed grid lines or construction marks. Type set at unconventional sizes (very large captions, small headings). Mixed alignment.
- **Safe fonts:** Fraktion Mono, GT America Mono, Nitti, ABC Arizona, Favorit
- **Logo style:** Typed or stenciled wordmark. Visible construction — the mark should look like it was built, not designed. Exposed baseline, visible grid, no polish.
- **Voice traits:** First person. Direct. Technical language unglossed. Incomplete sentences acceptable. References tools, materials, process. Reads like field notes.
- **Banned words:** "luxury," "premium," "elegant," "refined," "curated"
- **Texture:** Exposed materials — concrete, raw paper, bare metal, unfinished wood. If photography, process shots: hands at work, tools, mess. Film grain, visible noise.
- **Reference brands:** Ssense (editorial), Arena, 032c, Rick Owens

---

## 10. Lush

The brand indulges. Sensory richness across every surface.

- **OKLCH range:** L 25–50 (deep tones), C 40–80, H varied — jewel tones: H 0–20 (garnet), H 140–170 (emerald), H 260–290 (sapphire), H 310–340 (amethyst)
- **Color strategy:** Drenched — color IS the brand surface. Multiple saturated tones coexist.
- **Typography:** High-contrast serif with hairline thins and swelled thicks. Display italic for pull quotes. Generous leading (1.8+). Small caps for bylines.
- **Safe fonts:** Freight Display, Canela, Noe Display, Editorial New, Sang Bleu
- **Logo style:** Elegant wordmark, possibly with a custom ligature. Serif or high-contrast sans in a thin-to-thick style. If a mark exists, it's organic — botanical, figural, or calligraphic.
- **Voice traits:** Sensory adjectives earned by specificity ("Ceylon cinnamon" not "exotic spice"). Longer sentences with subordinate clauses. Second person when addressing the reader's senses. No tech jargon.
- **Banned words:** "innovative," "solutions," "optimize," "scalable," "leverage"
- **Texture:** Velvet, silk, lacquer, gloss. Deep color photography with rich blacks. If print: coated stock, spot varnish, metallic inks. Motion: slow, liquid, continuous.
- **Reference brands:** Diptyque, Tom Ford, Rolls-Royce, Maison Margiela (Replica line)

## Using This Reference

1. Interview produces 3–5 personality words
2. Map words to the closest archetype (often a blend of two)
3. Use the archetype's ranges as starting points, not destinations
4. Apply the AI slop test — if the result looks like anyone else in this archetype, push further
5. The banned-words lists are additive — combine lists from all relevant archetypes
6. Reference brands are for calibration, not imitation. If the output looks like one of them, it's too close
