# Brand Voice Development

Brand voice reference for Brand Studio Forge. Loaded in identity and content modes. Defines the voice/tone/style hierarchy, development process, and quality checks for all brand copy.

Voice is who the brand is. Tone is how the brand adapts to context. Style is the typographic and syntactic execution. Voice never changes. Tone shifts constantly. Confusing the two produces copy that sounds different every week.

---

## The Hierarchy

| Layer | What it governs | Changes? |
|---|---|---|
| **Voice** | Personality, worldview, vocabulary range, sentence rhythm | Never. It is the brand's character. |
| **Tone** | Formality, warmth, urgency, humor level | Per context: social ≠ legal ≠ packaging. |
| **Style** | Capitalization, punctuation, contractions, Oxford comma | Per medium, documented in a style sheet. |

**Named rule — The Constant Voice Principle:** If two pieces of copy from the same brand could have been written by different people with different personalities, the voice is undefined. Tone may differ. Voice must not.

---

## Voice Development Process

### 1. Audit

Collect every piece of existing copy: website, packaging, social, email, customer service scripts, internal docs. Map each against:

- **Foundation:** meaning, emphasis, clarity, consistency, positioning
- **Identification:** names, taglines, descriptors, product names
- **Navigation:** calls to action, signage, voicemail, email signatures
- **Information:** press releases, brochures, shareholder communications
- **Aspiration:** mission, vision, values, key messages, elevator pitch

Ask: does the brand sound like one person or a committee? If committee — the voice is broken.

### 2. Define

Write three **physical-object personality words** — words you could point at in a room. Not "modern" or "innovative." Words like *weathered*, *effervescent*, *angular*. These words become the voice filter.

Answer six brand questions in the brand's own voice:
1. Why are we here?
2. What do we do and how?
3. What makes us different?
4. Who are we for?
5. What do we value most?
6. What's our personality?

If you cannot answer each in under two sentences using the brand's voice, the voice is not defined yet.

### 3. Test

**Named rule — The Blind Attribution Test:** Remove the brand name and logo from a piece of copy. Show it to someone who knows the brand. If they cannot identify whose copy it is, the voice is not distinctive enough.

**Named rule — The Power of Three:** Every brand message should have one overarching statement supported by no more than three subordinate messages. People under cognitive load retain three points. A brand that tries to say four things says nothing.

### 4. Roll Out

Write a voice card — a single page anyone in the organization can hold while writing:

- 3 personality words
- 3 "We say / We don't say" pairs
- 1 sample paragraph in-voice
- The dos and don'ts list (below)

Pin it to the wall. If the voice card needs more than one page, the voice is too complicated to maintain.

## Voice Traits Framework

Define voice along these four axes. Each axis is a spectrum — pick a position, not a pole.

| Axis | Left pole | Right pole |
|---|---|---|
| **Formality** | Colloquial, first-name | Institutional, title-and-surname |
| **Warmth** | Intimate, personal | Detached, authoritative |
| **Energy** | Measured, deliberate | Urgent, kinetic |
| **Complexity** | Plain, monosyllabic | Rich, subordinate clauses |

A craft coffee brand might be: informal / warm / measured / rich.
A fintech might be: semi-formal / detached / urgent / plain.

Record the position. Any copy that drifts more than one notch on any axis has left the voice.

## Dos and Don'ts

### Do

- Write the way the brand's founder would talk at their best — clear, passionate, specific.
- Use sensory and concrete language. "Aged in whiskey barrels" beats "premium quality."
- Let sentence length vary. Short sentence for impact. Then a longer one to carry the reader through an idea that needs room.
- Write for the ear. If it sounds wrong read aloud, it is wrong.
- Make the customer the subject. "You" appears more often than "We."

### Don't

- Describe the brand's personality in copy. A funny brand is funny; it does not say "we have a sense of humor."
- Hedge. "We believe that perhaps our approach might help" — pick a position.
- Stack adjectives. One precise adjective outperforms three vague ones.
- Use jargon the customer wouldn't use in conversation with a friend.
- Begin sentences with "At [Brand Name], we..."

## Bans

These words are AI-branding tells, corporate filler, or both. Match-and-refuse.

| Word / Phrase | Why banned |
|---|---|
| elevate | Means nothing specific. |
| empower | Patronizing: implies the audience is powerless. |
| seamless | Every brand claims it. None deliver it. |
| leverage | Verb form of a noun nobody asked for. |
| innovative | Self-awarded medal. Show the innovation instead. |
| cutting-edge | Cliché since the 1990s. |
| transform | Promises more than copy can deliver. |
| unlock | Tech-startup filler word. |
| curated | Overused past the point of meaning. |
| holistic | Vague; say what is actually included. |
| synergy | Corporate jargon that erases specificity. |
| disrupt | Self-mythologizing. Let others call you disruptive. |
| best-in-class | Unverifiable superlative. |
| world-class | See above. |
| reimagine | Rebranding dressed as philosophy. |
| at the end of the day | Filler. Delete the phrase; the sentence improves. |
| it goes without saying | Then don't say it. |

**Named rule — The Substitution Test:** If you can swap a brand's copy with a competitor's and nobody notices, both brands have failed. Every word must earn its place by being specific to *this* brand's positioning, product, or personality.

---

## Voice Slop Test

If someone could read this copy and say "AI wrote that" or "any brand in this category could have written that," it fails.

**First-order check:** Could a competitor use the same sentence unchanged? If yes, the voice is generic.

**Second-order check:** Does the copy contain any word from the banned list? If yes, it is reflex-writing, not voice-writing.

**Third-order check:** Does the copy pass the Blind Attribution Test? If no, the voice has no signature.

---

## Permissions

- A brand voice **may** break grammar rules if the break is consistent and intentional (e.g., Innocent's lowercase, Apple's sentence fragments).
- A brand voice **may** coin vocabulary if the coined words are immediately understood and used consistently.
- A brand voice **may** adopt slang or dialect if the audience uses it naturally — never as decoration.
