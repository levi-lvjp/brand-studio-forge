# Brand Identity Process


---

## Slop Test

If your process skips research and jumps to color palettes, it's not branding. It's decoration.
If your "strategy" is three adjectives picked from a dropdown, it's not positioning. It's Mad Libs.
If every brand brief reads the same regardless of input, you're pattern-matching, not thinking.

---

## The Five Phases

### Phase 1: Conducting Research

Goal: understand the organization's mission, vision, values, target markets, competitive advantage, strengths, weaknesses, and challenges.

**Sub-steps:**
1. Clarify vision, strategies, goals, and values
2. Interview key management (see cross-ref: interview.md)
3. Research stakeholders' needs and perceptions
4. Conduct marketing, competitive, technology, legal, and message audits
5. Evaluate existing brands and brand architecture
6. Present audit readout with findings report

**Research principle:** "Research should inform future business decisions, not justify decisions already made."

**Defining the problem:** Research answers questions, but until the right questions are asked, useful answers remain elusive. Brainstorm, workshop, and iterate until aligned on the best way to define the problem.

### Phase 2: Clarifying Strategy

Goal: distill all learnings into a unifying idea and positioning strategy.

**Sub-steps:**
1. Synthesize learnings from Phase 1
2. Clarify brand strategy
3. Develop a positioning platform
4. Create brand attributes
5. Develop key messages
6. Write a brand brief (see Brand Brief Rule below)
7. Achieve agreement
8. Create a naming strategy (cross-ref: naming.md)
9. Write a creative brief

**Strategy flow:**
Understanding → Clarifying → Positioning → Brand essence → Big idea

The big idea can always be expressed in one sentence. It must carry enough ambiguity to allow for future developments, create an emotional connection, and be easy to talk about.

### Phase 3: Designing Identity

**Sub-steps:**
1. Visualize the future
2. Brainstorm the big idea
3. Design brand identity system
4. Explore key applications
5. Finalize brand architecture
6. Present visual strategy
7. Achieve agreement

### Phase 4: Creating Touchpoints

**Sub-steps:**
1. Finalize identity design
2. Develop look and feel
3. Initiate trademark protection
4. Prioritize and design applications
5. Develop system standards
6. Apply brand architecture

### Phase 5: Managing Assets

**Sub-steps:**
1. Build synergy around new strategy
2. Develop launch plan
3. Launch internally first, then externally
4. Develop standards and guidelines
5. Nurture brand champions

## The Five and a Half Steps

### Step 1: Investigate

Start by finding where the brand stands, or doesn't stand, in its market. The biggest temptation is declaring you know exactly what the problem is.

**Key principle:** "If you don't investigate, immerse yourself and carry out proper research, you'll end up creating fantastic solutions for the wrong problems."

**Five audit types:**
1. **Visual audit** — what existing identity looks like across surfaces
2. **Verbal audit** — words and phrases the brand uses
3. **Behavioural audit** — how employees speak, act, interact
4. **Competition audit** — visual + verbal + behavioural across competitors
5. **Peer audit** — aspirational organizations to benchmark against

### Step 2: Strategy and Narrative

Distill investigation into positioning and story. Strategy emerges from findings, not assumptions.

### Step 3: Design

Identity creation grounded in strategic clarity from steps 1-2.

### The Half Step: Bridging

The transition between investigation findings and creative execution. Prevents the fatal leap from brief to design without strategic grounding.

### Step 4: Expressing

Rolling the identity out across all touchpoints consistently.

### Step 5: Managing

Sustaining the brand over time with standards, training, and evolution.

## Named Rules

### The Brand Brief Rule

A brand brief is a one-page document, not a twenty-page treatise. Components:

| Component | What to Include |
|---|---|
| Core purpose / mission | Short, readable, memorable sentence or two |
| Audience | Target audience, highest-level needs, aspirations, challenges |
| Value proposition | Functional, emotional, and social benefits |
| Values | Selective words to describe brand values |
| Personality attributes | Selective words for voice and distinctive character |
| Key competitors | Points of difference and parity |
| Business/product/services | Top three to four offers |
| Proof points | Irrefutable evidence to support value proposition |
| Big idea | Succinct, memorable phrase |

### The Critical Success Factors Rule

Before starting, confirm four factors:
1. The CEO supports this initiative
2. The company is ready to invest time, resources, and brainpower
3. There is an endpoint everyone understands and agrees on
4. There is value to the outcome, and all agree what success looks like

### The Findings Report Rule

The findings report is a formal presentation synthesizing interviews, audits, and research. It sets the stage for the strategy phase. Organize findings across: interviews, brand essence, marketing research, marketing audit, and verbal audit.

---

## Bans

- Do not skip Phase 1. Research is not optional.
- Do not write a brand brief before the brand brief has been approved by stakeholders. ("Never write it until the brand brief has been approved.")
- Do not confuse personal aesthetics with functional criteria.
- Do not use focus groups as a replacement for the decision-making process.

---

## Permissions

- You may compress phases for small brands. Phases 1+2 can merge into a single discovery session if the business is small enough for one person to represent all stakeholder views.
- You may run audits in parallel. Visual, verbal, competitive, and behavioural audits are independent.
- You may start Phase 3 before Phase 2 is fully documented, as long as the brand brief is approved.
